Uploadable makes a photo meet an upload requirement: the right shape, the right pixel dimensions, inside the size range the form asks for, as a JPEG in sRGB with the location data removed. Everything below is how it works and what to do when it does something you did not expect.

Making a photo fit

Open Uploadable, tap Select Photo, choose the destination your form belongs to, place the crop, and tap Make it fit. You can also share a photo to Uploadable from Photos, Files, or any app with a share button.

If the destination you need is not listed, choose Custom and type the numbers your form asks for.

Placing the crop

Choosing a destination reveals a crop rectangle over your photo, locked to the shape that destination requires. Drag it to move, pinch to resize.

This step is worth taking care over. A square crop centred on a portrait photo will usually clip the top of someone's head, and the resulting file is correct in every measurable way while being the wrong part of the photograph. Uploadable will not guess at framing on your behalf — it does not look at the picture at all.

Which is why the crop screen tells you what to look for rather than judging it: forms want your whole head inside the frame, with a little space above and below the chin. If it will not fit at any crop size, the photograph was taken too close and no amount of cropping will fix it — you need one taken from further back. We cannot tell you that about your own photo, because we never look at it. You can see it at a glance.

Where your photo goes

Save to Photos puts it in your photo library. Save to Files puts it in Files → On My iPhone → Uploadable. Share hands it to any app you choose.

One thing that surprises people. A photo saved to Files from the share sheet shows up in Files → Uploadable only after you next open the Uploadable app. This is an iOS rule, not a choice: a share extension runs in its own sandbox and is not permitted to write into the app's Files folder. The extension saves your photo straight away and the app files it the next time it runs. Nothing is lost in between.

What the green ticks mean

The result screen lists each thing the form asked for and confirms it. Those are not predictions — Uploadable re-reads the finished file from disk and checks it: the pixel dimensions, the byte count, that it really is a JPEG, that the sRGB profile is embedded, and that the EXIF and location data are gone. If any check fails, the file is discarded rather than handed to you.

"Verified" dates

Each destination shows the date its requirements were last read off the official government page, and the one you have chosen shows a link to that page, under the crop. Governments change these numbers without announcing it, and a stale number would turn a portal's rejection into our fault. If a date looks old, check the official page — the link is right there.

Destinations whose requirements could not be confirmed are not offered at all. A specification that rules nothing out would happily approve a screenshot.

Notes beside the ticks

Sometimes a result passes and there is still something worth telling you.

  • Smaller than other forms require. A few destinations state no minimum size at all, so a small photo technically satisfies them. If your result is smaller than every other form we know of asks for, Uploadable says so — particularly where the portal crops the photo again after upload, which leaves fewer pixels still.
  • Compressed harder than we'd like. Fitting a large photo into a tight size limit costs quality. Uploadable reduces the dimensions first to protect it, and tells you if it still had to compress firmly.

Neither is a failure. The file meets the stated requirements either way.

When it refuses

Uploadable will not produce a file it expects to be rejected. If your photo is 400 × 400 and the form needs at least 600 × 600, it says exactly that and stops, rather than enlarging it — enlarging invents detail that is not there, and forms check for it. The same goes for a size range it cannot reach: you will be told the numbers rather than handed a file that fails.

What Uploadable does not do

It does not edit your photograph. No filters, no retouching, no background removal, no face detection, no AI of any kind. It changes the file — the format, the dimensions, the colour profile, the metadata — and never the picture.

That is deliberate. Government photo pages ask you not to alter your appearance and say they screen for it. Uploadable is a file tool, and it stays on the right side of that line.

It also cannot tell you whether your photograph itself will be accepted. Head size, expression, lighting and background are the photography problem, and no file tool can judge them.

Free exports and Uploadable Pro

Fitting photos is free and unlimited — you can run any photo against any destination and see the full result. What is metered is getting the file out: the first two exports are free, and after that Uploadable Pro is $9.99, paid once. No subscription, no account.

Saving one result to Photos and then to Files and then sharing it counts as one export, not three.

Restoring your purchase

Sign in to the same Apple Account you bought it with, open Uploadable, and tap Restore Purchases on the Pro screen. The purchase is attached to your Apple Account, so it comes back on any device you own.
